# Orbit Abroad — Customer Launch Kit

## First-market definition

- **Audience:** International students and recent expats living in the United States
- **Stage:** Free private beta
- **Core job:** Turn unfamiliar notices into reviewed tasks with deadlines and useful details
- **Promise:** “Find the deadline, amount, and next action—without uploading the document.”
- **Boundary:** Orbit organizes information; it does not provide immigration, legal, financial, medical, tax, or government advice.

## Ideal first tester

Recruit someone who:

- moved to the United States within the last three years;
- personally handles utilities, school paperwork, insurance, identification, or appointments;
- has felt uncertain about an official notice or nearly missed a deadline; and
- can test with invented or carefully redacted text.

Do not recruit minors for this beta. Do not ask testers to share unredacted documents.

## Outreach templates

Send these manually only to people or communities where outreach is welcome. Personalize the first sentence and do not mass-message.

### Direct message

> Hi [name] — I’m testing a private tool for people handling unfamiliar U.S. paperwork after moving countries. It turns redacted notice text into a task with the deadline, amount, and next action. Nothing is uploaded. Would you be open to a 15-minute test using two sample notices? I’m looking for honest feedback, not a positive review.

> Thank you for testing Orbit Abroad. Your feedback about [specific observation] was especially useful. If you use it again this week, please note anything you distrust, misunderstand, or wish it handled differently. There is a Feedback button inside the app that downloads a note you can send to me.

## 15-minute interview script

### Before showing the product

1. What kinds of paperwork became harder after moving to the United States?
2. How do you currently track deadlines from notices or emails?
3. Tell me about the last time you nearly missed something important.
4. Which information would you never paste into a tool?

Do not pitch or explain Orbit while asking these questions.

### Usability task

Say:

> Imagine this utility notice arrived today. Please use Orbit to decide what you need to do. Think aloud, and stop if anything feels unsafe or unclear.

Observe without helping:

- whether the person notices the redaction warning;
- whether they understand that analysis is local;
- whether they check the extracted fields;
- which wording creates hesitation;
- whether they can find the task after saving; and
- whether they understand backup and deletion.

### After the task

1. What did you expect to happen before you clicked “Find the task”?
2. Which extracted field did you trust least?
3. When would you use this in real life?
4. What would stop you from using it?
5. Would you be disappointed if you could not use it next week? Why?
6. What would make this worth paying for later?

## Session safety protocol

1. Begin with a built-in demo notice.
2. If the tester wants to use real text, ask them to redact it first.
3. Never request, record, photograph, or retain the original notice.
4. Do not collect Social Security, passport, banking, card, login, authentication, or unnecessary medical details.
5. Do not interpret immigration status or advise what action is legally required.
6. Ask permission before taking anonymous notes.
7. Store research notes separately from any identifying contact information.

## Beta success signals

The product is demonstrating value when testers:

- complete onboarding without help;
- create a task from a notice;
- catch and correct uncertain suggestions;
- return with another notice;
- export a backup or calendar reminder;
- explain the privacy model accurately; and
- ask to retain access after the test.

Treat praise as weak evidence. Repeated use and behavior are stronger evidence.

## Launch sequence

### Before inviting anyone

- Obtain Cameron’s approval to publish or deploy.
- Replace any deployment-specific metadata with the final HTTPS domain.
- Have the privacy notice and beta terms reviewed for the intended launch context.
- Test the deployed PWA installation and offline behavior over HTTPS.
- Use only invented/redacted screenshots and examples.

### First cohort

- Invite 5–10 adult design partners individually.
- Run observed sessions before sharing the app for independent use.
- Fix trust, comprehension, and incorrect-date issues before adding features.
- Review feedback after every few sessions.

### Expansion gate

Broaden acquisition only after users repeatedly return and extraction corrections are understood. Do not add payment collection until the value and support burden are clear.
